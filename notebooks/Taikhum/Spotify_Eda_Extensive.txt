-> During Silver validation, 78 Spotify track URIs (≈0.03% of unique songs) were found to have multiple track names due to source metadata inconsistencies (capitalization or naming changes). Since the URI remains stable and uniquely identifies the song, no transformation was applied.

If the examiner asks
Why didn't you standardize track names?

You can confidently answer:
Spotify URI is the canonical identifier for a track. Track names are descriptive metadata and may legitimately change due to capitalization, punctuation, localization, or metadata updates. Therefore, the ETL preserves the original source values while using the URI as the business key for all aggregations.

-> Metadata Enrichment Feasibility Study

Before finalizing the Silver layer, a metadata enrichment experiment was conducted by joining the chart fact data with the master songs.csv dataset. The objective was to determine whether the master dataset could improve track names, artist names, labels, and release dates. Although the master dataset contained cleaner metadata, it covered only 85.65% of the chart songs and provided minimal recovery of missing release dates (302 rows) and labels (1 row). In addition, substantial differences were observed in track names and record labels, indicating business-level metadata variations rather than simple formatting inconsistencies. Based on these findings, the Silver layer retained the chart metadata as the authoritative source, while the master datasets were preserved as reference dimensions for future enrichment and expansion.

-> Song Lifecycle Classification: Songs were categorized into four lifecycle stages based on their age relative to the chart date: New Release (0–90 days), Recent Hit (91–365 days), Established (1–5 years), and Evergreen (>5 years). This classification enables analysis of catalog freshness, long-term popularity, and market preference for new versus legacy content, supporting executive decision-making in the Market Opportunity Dashboard.
